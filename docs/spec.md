## Problem Statement

A Business needs a small public digital business card that Visitors can open by tapping NFC, scanning a QR code, or following a sharing link. Its printed Card destination must survive changes to Business name, page content, slug and Card assignment. NexTap Admin needs to manage many Businesses and permanent Cards centrally, transfer Cards safely, control publication, and understand successful lifecycle changes without exposing private operational information.

The central consistency problem is keeping those permanent destinations useful while honoring current publication and access decisions. A Disabled Business, a transferred or Inactive Card, a replaced image, a stale edit, or a failed dependency must not expose an old assignment or silently overwrite current state. Caching and retries must preserve current eligibility and committed truth. Visitors need clear safe notices and direct contact or external navigation; they do not need a login, payment processing or a full website builder.

This is a documentation-only synthesis of the five sources identified in Further Notes. It specifies intended behavior and distinguishes settled policies from technical defaults; it does not establish an implementation, tested provider configuration or deployment authorization.

## Solution

NexTap is one multi-tenant application at `nextap.services`, with NexTap Admin as the only authenticated launch role. A **Business** is the tenant/content boundary. A **Card** is a physical card with one immutable public **Card ID**, separate from its internal UUID. A Business can have many Cards; a Card has at most one current **Assignment**. **Activation** changes an assigned Card to Active and is independent of enabling its Business.

An eligible `https://nextap.services/c/{Card ID}` request renders the current public Business page directly at that permanent URL. The independent `/b/{slug}` sharing route renders an Enabled Business. A former **Business slug** becomes a permanently reserved **Slug alias** that redirects only to the same Business after a fresh eligibility check. Every new public page or image request checks current authorization to publish before using cached content. Unknown eligibility fails closed.

NexTap Admin provisions Businesses, imports and manages Card inventory, assigns and separately activates Cards, edits content, explicitly enables or disables Businesses, and reads minimal lifetime **Card history**. Businesses start Disabled; Cards start Inactive and unassigned. Enabled content saves publish subsequent requests without a separate draft workflow. Deactivate preserves assignment; Unassign clears assignment and leaves the Card Inactive. Transfer requires Deactivate, reassign while Inactive, then separate Activate.

A responsive **public Business page** uses at most one of each registered **Section**: Hero, Contact, Social, Payments, Location, Hours, Reviews and Custom Links. It supports authoritative contact fields, external **Links**, weekly hours with a Business timezone, optional Logo and Cover, and configured map and Google Reviews destinations. Payment actions are external HTTPS navigation only. Required authentication, isolation, validation, atomic history/audit, version checks and durable retry handling protect Admin operations.

## Implementation Decisions

**Decision status and ownership.** Product scope, lifecycle and vocabulary are settled product policy. Architecture owns runtime, cache and recovery mechanics; Database owns persistence, constraints and transaction shape; Flows and API owns transport; Security and edge cases owns enforcement, validation, privacy and retention. Details explicitly called defaults, proposed or recommended below remain technical design choices within those policies. They are not additional approved product decisions, completed implementation or new external gates. The modules described here are logical responsibilities in one application, not existing source modules.

1. **Application and environment boundaries — settled stack, recommended module structure.** Use one Next.js application on Cloudflare Workers with Supabase PostgreSQL, Auth and private Storage. Staging and production require separate Workers and Supabase services. Recommended internal modules cover HTTP routing, current authorization, Admin management, restricted eligibility/projection reads, public rendering, private image handling and bounded maintenance. Route handlers parse and respond; management/database transactions alone change tenant state, references and required records. Renderer/cache code does not authorize access or mutate tenant data. Separate domains, issuers, databases, buckets, secrets, cache namespaces, rate counters, retry records and maintenance state are recommended isolation details. Staging uses synthetic content and separately provisioned Admins.

2. **Identity and persistence — settled invariants with physical defaults.** Internal Cards, Businesses and content use UUIDs. Card IDs are globally unique, immutable public tokens, never credentials or recovery authority. New tokens require at least 128 cryptographically random bits; their exact encoding is not selected. Imported printed tokens remain exact, including case and surrounding whitespace in decoded CSV values. Proposed exact token comparison is binary-safe and case-sensitive; no new-token regex applies to legacy inventory before verification. PostgreSQL is the system of record. The physical design includes current Admin membership, Businesses, Cards, a global slug registry, Sections, Links, weekly hours/intervals, private image references, lifetime Card history, minimized audit, retry records and temporary import/upload records. There is no Business-owner foreign key. Server/database time supplies UTC timestamps, actor attribution and lifecycle times. Persisted edit versions and content revisions start at 1; they are distinct positive counters even though the generic transport convention calls them non-negative.

3. **Card lifecycle — settled policy.** Cards start Inactive and unassigned. Assign/reassign requires Inactive and leaves Inactive; Activate separately requires an assignment; Deactivate retains assignment; Unassign clears it and leaves Inactive. An assigned Card can be Active while its Business is Disabled. Enforce at most one current assignment, an Active-implies-assigned constraint, immutable tokens and restricted deletion. The API's defensive unavailable response for an Active unassigned Card does not make that a valid persisted state. No Card deletion, token reuse or Business hard deletion is available.

4. **Business publication — settled policy and explicit transport constraint.** Businesses start Disabled. Explicit enablement requires only a nonempty name and a unique current slug; contact, Links, hours, location and images remain optional. Enabled content edits publish subsequent requests. Disable preserves content, aliases, Card assignment and Card status while denying all associated public routes. Product says name is required only for enablement; the current Database design and API creation contract also require a nonempty name and slug at creation and reject null name/slug edits. Preserve that documented technical contract without recasting creation-time name validation as a settled product requirement. An unnamed Disabled draft is therefore not a defined API flow; relaxing the technical contract would require coherent persistence/API revision, not an invented nullable payload here.

5. **Slug registry — settled reservation policy, proposed normalization.** Store one current slug and permanent historical aliases tied to the same Business in a globally unique registry. Reserve a new current slug and retire the prior one atomically with Business version/revision and audit. Conflicts abort the whole change. The documented default trims Unicode whitespace, applies NFC and lowercase, then accepts only ASCII lowercase alphanumeric groups separated by single hyphens, with a 1–80 character bound. Reject empty/reserved values, leading/trailing or repeated hyphens and incompatible characters; do not manufacture a valid slug by removing hyphens or transliterating. Never apply slug normalization to Card IDs. Pre-existing slug compatibility remains an implementation check within this default.

6. **Public HTTP contract — API-owned.** `GET /c/{cardId}` renders eligible current Business HTML directly with 200. A known Inactive Card returns 200 HTML containing exactly “This NexTap card has not been activated yet.” without assignment, history, UUID or Admin detail. Unknown Cards and Active Cards with unavailable publication return safe 404 HTML; unavailable required eligibility/content reads return safe 503 HTML. `GET /b/{slug}` returns 200 for an Enabled current slug, 404 for missing/non-disclosed/Disabled state, and 503 for unavailable eligibility. An eligible historical alias returns 308 with the current Business route as its Location; the destination checks eligibility again. Unknown eligibility never produces a stale redirect. Exact notice wording beyond the Inactive notice is not supplied.

7. **Fresh eligibility and coherent content — settled cache policy, recommended mechanics.** Every new Card, Business, alias and image request establishes authoritative current eligibility before shared-cache reuse. Card reads include current assignment/status and Business publication; image reads include current image reference. No replica lag, cached authorization or stale fallback may substitute. Content-affecting Business changes atomically advance content revision once. Recommended cache keys include environment, Business UUID, content revision, renderer version and schema version. Cache entries contain only restricted public content, never cached Card mappings or eligibility. On a miss/read failure, load a coherent restricted projection that rechecks the originating route, Business identity, eligibility and revision. If the route becomes an alias, return the freshly gated redirect. If identity/revision changes during loading, use the new coherent tuple with at most one retry or return 503; never cache mixed rows under an earlier tuple. Cache-write failure cannot fail an otherwise valid read. Publication requires no global purge and has no delivery-pending state.

8. **HTTP/cache bypass protection — enforcement requirement with header default.** Fresh checks precede conditional responses, HEAD, redirects, framework prefetch/navigation and image optimization. Static generation, ISR, route/data caches, CDN rules and direct Storage access cannot become alternate ungated delivery paths. Recommended HTTP behavior is `Cache-Control: private, no-store` for public HTML, aliases, notices, gated images and Admin/session responses; internal projection/immutable-byte caching remains behind the gate. A stale ETag cannot authorize a 304 after revocation. Already delivered, authorized in-flight, downloaded or browser-cached copies cannot be recalled.

9. **Page composition and authoritative content — settled product model.** Permit at most one Hero, Contact, Social, Payments, Location, Hours, Reviews and Custom Links Section per Business. Sections are registered configurable content groups, not executable tenant components. Phone, WhatsApp and email are authoritative contact values used to derive validated contact actions. Location uses canonical address, map URL and directions URL; Reviews uses the canonical Google Reviews URL. The latter storage mapping is a Database/API default. Null means no configured value/action; an address need not have map destinations. Section settings contain presentation only, never canonical contact/location/review fields, hours, Links, storage paths or payment identifiers. Public projections exclude hidden Section content, inactive Links, Admin identities, internal operational IDs, inventory, history, audit and private Storage keys. Authorized Business detail reads include hidden content for editing.

10. **Sections, Links and order — settled behavior with registry defaults.** Generic link-bearing Sections are Social, Payments and Custom Links. Each Link belongs to exactly one supported Section of its Business, with a same-Business composite relationship constraint. Contact-type generic Links are rejected; Location and Reviews use canonical fields instead. At most 50 generic Links per Business includes inactive Links and concurrent inserts. Complete Section order lists include every configured Section, even disabled ones; complete Section-local Link orders include every member, even inactive ones. Reject omissions, duplicates, deleted IDs, unsupported types and cross-Business/other-Section IDs. Persist atomically; render enabled Sections and active member Links in retained relative order with stable ID tie-breaking. No hidden Link moves to a fallback list. Proposed normalized-label uniqueness applies to active Links within one Business and Section using trimmed NFC/case-folded comparison; inactive duplicates may remain but activation must pass the check. Display casing is retained.

11. **Settings and type registry — proposed technical defaults.** The initial settings schema is version 1 with an empty-object default. All listed keys are optional; omission selects the renderer default. Reject explicit null, nesting, additional keys, wrong types and mismatched schema versions. `title` is 1–80 trimmed NFC Unicode scalar values when present. Hero, Location and Hours permit title only; Contact, Payments, Reviews and Custom Links also permit `layout` with the sole value `buttons`; Social also permits `style` as `icons` by default or `buttons`. The proposed Social Link registry permits instagram, facebook, tiktok, youtube, snapchat, linkedin and x; Payments permits payment, instapay, vodafone_cash, paypal and stripe; Custom Links permits custom. Icons are null or a registered type key, never URLs, CSS or tenant artwork. Additional types/icons require application/schema-registry support. Proposed settings measurement is compact UTF-8 serialized JSON, at most 8,192 bytes, not the database's internal JSON storage size.

12. **Weekly hours — settled feature, Database/API representation default.** Support an optional complete seven-day schedule and IANA Business timezone, defaulting to UTC on creation. Weekdays are 0 Monday through 6 Sunday. A supplied schedule includes each weekday exactly once; an empty intervals list means closed. Absence of a schedule differs from a configured all-closed week. Null clears the schedule; supplying one replaces it atomically. Local opening times range from 00:00 to 23:59 and closing times additionally allow 24:00. Reject equal endpoints and overlap; accept and preserve adjacent intervals. An earlier closing time denotes overnight input, split at midnight onto the next weekday, including Sunday-to-Monday wrap, before overlap validation. Reads return same-day intervals ordered by opening time. Persisted hours timezone equals Business timezone; changing timezone validates retained/supplied hours in the same Business content transaction. No holiday calendar or open-now calculation is included.

13. **Content validation — accepted bounds, labeled normalization defaults.** Require plain text and HTTPS web destinations without embedded credentials. Derive validated telephone, email and WhatsApp actions from canonical contact fields. Do not execute HTML/scripts or fetch supplied URLs server-side. Accepted maxima are 120 characters for name, 1,000 for description, 80 for Link label, 2,048 for destination URL, 50 generic Links per Business and 8 KiB settings per Section. Proposed details are NFC normalization, trimming surrounding Unicode whitespace and counting Unicode scalar values; a 500-character address bound; and a 256 KiB ceiling for each non-multipart JSON request. Optional empty text/contact values normalize to null. Phone/WhatsApp retain only an optional leading plus and ASCII digits after permitted spaces, hyphens and parentheses are removed; malformed values are rejected. Email retains local-part casing and lowercases the domain after trim/NFC validation. Printed Card tokens are exempt from every normalization step. HTTPS validation does not establish a destination operator's trustworthiness.

14. **Images — settled access/validation policy, recommended crash-safe workflow.** Logo and Cover are optional, with at most one current reference per role. Accept actual JPEG, PNG or WebP bytes only, at most 2 MiB and 16 megapixels; decode/re-encode, strip metadata and reject SVG, animation and malformed data with bounded decoder resources. MIME labels and extensions alone are insufficient. Register a server-owned immutable upload intent before private object storage, bound to actor, Business, role, digest and expiry. After storage succeeds, reauthorize, lock intent and Business, check unused/unexpired state and expected version, then atomically switch reference, consume intent, update version/revision, audit, record retry result and mark the old object for cleanup. Failure before reference commit preserves the previous image; failure after commit cannot delete the new object to simulate rollback. Removal uses the same reference transaction without an upload. Public locators are opaque image identities, never private paths; every new delivery proves current Logo/Cover ownership by an Enabled Business. Separate authorized Admin preview delivery does not enter a shared response cache. Exact image URL syntax is not defined in these sources.

15. **Image maintenance — proposed mechanics and deletion deadline.** Replaced/removed images become inaccessible and eligible for deletion immediately at reference commit. Physical deletion within seven days is a proposed maximum cleanup deadline, not a public-access or minimum-retention grace. Raw imports and unused temporary uploads expire within the accepted 24 hours. Track upload intents and cleanup candidates, using bounded scheduled maintenance in the same application. Lock and expire unused intents before deleting so completion cannot race cleanup. Prove no current reference uses the object; cleanup candidates cannot be reattached as current. Missing objects count as successful deletion; provider failures remain retryable with redacted alerts. Reconcile crashes and uncertain storage results using server-owned identity. Never use age-only bucket deletion. Storage bytes and PostgreSQL references do not share a transaction.

16. **Authentication and authorization — settled controls with enforcement defaults.** Provision individual Supabase email/password Admin identities with mandatory authenticator-app TOTP and no public signup/shared credential. Enforce verified JWT signature, issuer/project, audience and expiry, plus authoritative current Admin membership and server-enforced 8-hour absolute/30-minute idle session expiry on every privileged request. Do not trust editable profile roles, unverified claims or cached membership. Recommended idle renewal occurs only for an accepted request and never extends absolute expiry. Enforce MFA in API, database and server rendering. Reassignment, Business enablement, payment-link changes and Admin-permission changes require TOTP completed within five minutes. The proposed freshness record is server-observed, session/Admin-bound and based on a single-use challenge; missing/unreadable/expired freshness fails closed. Bootstrap and lost-factor recovery require a trusted operator, out-of-band identity verification and audit attribution. Password reset cannot create membership or silently remove MFA; Card ID possession grants no authority.

17. **Isolation and browser request protection — settled controls.** NexTap Admin manages all Businesses; tenant isolation does not limit a platform Admin to one Business. Use identity-scoped management clients, restrictive grants/RLS and explicit stored/requested child relationship validation within the mutation. Deny anonymous/unrelated identities management tables, child metadata, history, audit, retry records and private objects. Public reads expose only a restricted projection. Scoped server-only privileged credentials need explicit Admin, Business/resource and operation checks; normal management cannot depend on unrestricted service credentials. Exposed views/functions must not bypass RLS accidentally. Cookie-authenticated mutations require CSRF protection and Origin/Referer validation; the default is a synchronizer token or equivalent same-origin control. SameSite alone and CORS are not authorization. Verify direct provider paths separately because edge protection does not automatically cover them.

18. **Admin HTTP surface — settled methods with API-owned routes.** Use same-origin private HTTP/JSON under `/api/admin`, internal canonical lowercase UUID references, JSON content types and RFC 3339 UTC times. Every response carries an opaque `requestId` header; errors repeat it. Reject client actor IDs, roles, audit timestamps, raw Storage paths and server-controlled versions except `expectedVersion`. Session, Card and Business list/detail reads, per-Card history and operation reconciliation use GET. Business creation uses POST to the Businesses collection and returns 201 Disabled; Business fields use PATCH and explicit enable/disable use POST commands. Card assign/unassign/activate/deactivate are explicit POST commands beneath the Card resource. Sections use PATCH by type and PUT for complete order. Links use POST creation, PATCH editing, DELETE removal and PUT for complete Section-local order. Images use multipart PUT by Logo/Cover role and DELETE removal. CSV preview and commit have separate POST operations. Pure reads and preview are not effectful commands. A separate single-Card creation route, Admin-permission/recovery route payloads, and public JSON page API are not supplied; do not invent them.

19. **Business and resource payloads — API-owned defaults.** Business creation requires name and slug, with optional description, phone, WhatsApp, email, address, mapUrl, directionsUrl, googleReviewsUrl, timezone and hours. PATCH uses the same fields plus expected version: omitted fields stay unchanged and nullable fields accept null to clear. No separate hours/location/reviews mutation route is needed. Section updates accept enabled state and registered settings; Section order uses section types. Link creation identifies Section type, registered type, label, URL, optional icon and active state; Link order identifies Section and complete member IDs. Image replacement uploads the file with expected Business version and idempotency key in the documented multipart/header form. Link/image removals carry expected version through the documented query/body convention. Child-content operations use the parent Business version. Card summaries expose internal ID, public Card ID, status, nullable Business ID and activation time, creation time and version to authorized Admins. Business summaries expose ID, name, current slug, status, content revision, timestamps and version. Raw image paths and credentials are excluded.

20. **Lists and history transport — proposed API defaults.** Lists return data, nextCursor and requestId, with a default limit of 50 and a maximum of 100. Opaque base64url cursors bind a server-signed sort tuple to the query. Cards and Businesses sort by creation time then ID ascending; Card history sorts by occurrence time then ID ascending. A descending history index in the Database design does not change that API order. Invalid signatures, resource/query mismatch or expired signing keys return 422 invalid_cursor. Traversal has no snapshot guarantee; concurrent rows/changes may appear on later traversal. Bounded case-insensitive prefix/substring search covers Card ID or Business name/slug; this search behavior never changes exact token identity. Cards support status and Business filters; Businesses support status. Unknown filters are semantic validation errors, and arbitrary/sensitive sort keys are not accepted.

21. **Versions, transaction boundaries and history — settled guarantees, recommended locking.** Serialize Card writes on the Card and child/content writes on the parent Business; compare expected version inside the transaction. A stale write returns 409 version_conflict with safe resource ID/current version/request ID and changes no resource, history, successful audit, reference or revision. A successful mutation increments aggregate edit version once; content-affecting changes also increment content revision once. Public scalar/contact/location/review fields, timezone/hours, Sections, Links, order, image references and current-slug changes affect content revision. Card lifecycle, membership and audit-only changes do not alter shared Business content revision; pure enable/disable need not do so. Successful state, minimized audit, required Card history and durable original retry result commit together. Required audit/history failure rolls back and returns 503. Card history records actual created/imported/assigned/reassigned/activated/deactivated/unassigned events with actor UUID, database time, old/new status and Business, and mutation correlation; creation/import old state may be null. It is append-only, read-only to Admin and retained for Card lifetime, without IPs, copied emails, content snapshots, Visitor events or reason text. Rejected attempts may enter separate minimized security audit, not successful lifecycle history.

22. **No-op lifecycle commands — explicit API contract.** An authorized, version-matching no-op returns 200 with unchanged representation, `committed:false` and `noop:true`. It does not increment version or append history or successful mutation audit. This does not bypass current authorization, sensitive-action requirements or expected-version checks. The source defines this specifically for Card commands; it does not establish a universal identical-field-save no-op policy for every content endpoint.

23. **Idempotency and reconciliation — settled replay policy, detailed implementation default.** Every mutation, including PATCH/PUT/DELETE, Business creation/lifecycle, Card commands, Link/image changes and CSV commit, requires an Idempotency-Key; GET and CSV preview do not. The default accepts 1–128 printable ASCII characters and stores a digest rather than the key. Bind operation scope, authenticated Admin, exact target/resource and canonical payload or upload digest. The database composite uniqueness key is supplemented by API-required serialization of the same actor/key across operation scopes: reuse with a different scope, target or payload returns 409 and cannot execute anew. Another Admin cannot replay the original Admin's result. Matching concurrent requests wait within a bounded limit and replay the original result; unavailable completion can return retryable 503, while conflicting concurrent requests return the documented in-progress/reused-key conflict. Replay rechecks current authentication, membership, MFA and required fresh TOTP before returning the stored original result with `replayed:true`; it does not rerun the mutation or reapply its old expected version as a new write. Retain completed/terminal results for the proposed 24 hours after completion/rejection. Database's proposed 30-minute processing-record duration is a reconciliation default, not permission to delete an active record or rerun an uncertain operation. On key expiry, reread state before deciding on a new request under normal lifecycle/version checks.

24. **Committed-result truth — API contract.** Committed or replayable mutation results include operationId, applicable version/contentRevision, committed/replayed indicators and requestId. `committed:true` establishes the database transaction and required records, not delivery, cleanup or external navigation success. If a response is lost, retry the same key/payload during retention. If unavailable, an authenticated GET of the operation ID returns the stored result for the current Admin without rerunning it; non-disclosed/missing operation returns 404 and unavailable operation store returns 503. If the original ID was never received, reread resource version/content revision. Do not blindly choose a new key or infer rollback from timeout. Cache fill, browser delivery and object cleanup occur outside the commit and cannot reverse it. No separate guaranteed operation-result retention longer than retry retention is specified.

25. **CSV preview and atomic import — settled behavior with exact parsing defaults.** Accept UTF-8 CSV, optionally with a UTF-8 BOM, and RFC 4180 quoting. Headers are case-sensitive after BOM removal and contain exactly card_id plus optional status. Ignore blank lines after the header. Preserve the nonempty decoded card_id field exactly, including surrounding whitespace; quoting is syntax, not token content. Reject malformed CSV, invalid UTF-8 and tokens incompatible with verified legacy grammar instead of repairing them. Optional status permits only Inactive, accepting case-insensitive spelling and reporting canonical spelling. Limit input to 10 MiB and 10,000 data rows; preview is valid for 30 minutes. Preview returns its reference, expiry, row/created/existing counts and row/field errors. Any validation error or intra-file duplicate prevents commit. Bind preview to Admin, exact digest, parsed rows and expiry; commit rechecks current authorization/MFA, integrity, expiry, validation and uniqueness. Existing exact tokens remain unchanged and are reported as skipped, including concurrent inserts after preview. New Cards are Inactive/unassigned; all new rows, attributed import history, audit and retry result commit atomically. Success reports created/skipped records and counts with committed result metadata. Preview has no inventory/lifecycle effect; temporary preview persistence is addressed under source reconciliation in Further Notes.

26. **Error contract — accepted envelope and API-owned statuses.** Admin errors contain error code, safe message, optional fieldErrors and requestId. Omit fieldErrors when inapplicable; UI translation is separate. Missing and non-disclosed resources share 404. Do not expose secrets, stack traces, other tenant data, private changes or the winning Admin in a conflict. Use the following documented map:

| HTTP status | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| 400 | invalid_request: malformed JSON, multipart, missing body or invalid path syntax |
| 401 | authentication_required: missing or expired session |
| 403 | admin_required, mfa_required or csrf_failed |
| 404 | not_found: missing or non-disclosed resource; safe unavailable public state |
| 409 | version_conflict, invalid_transition, idempotency_key_reused, idempotency_key_in_progress, slug_conflict or token_conflict |
| 413 | payload_too_large |
| 415 | unsupported_media_type |
| 422 | validation_failed, invalid_cursor, preview_expired or preview_mismatch |
| 429 | rate_limited, with Retry-After seconds when known |
| 503 | temporarily_unavailable: required dependency or required audit/history unavailable |
| 500 | internal_error without sensitive details |

27. **Abuse protection — accepted launch limits.** Login/recovery allows at most 10 attempts per 15 minutes and MFA 5 per 10 minutes, using account and trusted-IP controls. Admin mutations allow 120 per 10 minutes per Admin; uploads 20 per 10 minutes per Admin; inventory imports 5 per hour per Admin. Public routes receive volumetric protection without a low blanket Visitor quota. Choose bounded enforcement storage and trusted proxy identity during implementation. Missing rate-store/trusted-proxy guarantees fail closed or conservatively deny privileged/authentication mutations rather than disabling limits. Transient account/IP rate context does not authorize retained Visitor analytics. Direct Supabase Auth/Data/Storage paths need their own effective authorization and applicable provider limits.

28. **Minimization and retention — settled periods versus defaults.** Keep routine minimized Admin audit for 90 days, redacted operational errors for 14 days, raw imports/unused temporary uploads for 24 hours and minimal Card history for permanent Card lifetime. Preview validity is 30 minutes. Completed retry-result retention of 24 hours and replaced-image physical cleanup within seven days are separately labeled technical defaults. Error monitoring retains request ID, route template and redacted error only; exclude retained Visitor identifiers/IPs, full public tokens, queries, bodies, credentials and tracking. Audit excludes raw tokens/bodies/secrets, copied emails, IPs and session tokens. Provider-native logs, Auth metadata, Storage versions and backups may behave differently; application expiry does not prove provider deletion.

29. **Migrations and recovery — accepted review boundary, recommended procedure.** Use reviewed versioned Supabase SQL migrations, rehearsed in staging with synthetic data; prohibit undocumented dashboard-only production schema changes. Preserve printed tokens, aliases, constraints and lifetime history. Prefer additive compatible expansion, validate/backfill existing values before restrictive constraints, and verify application/schema coexistence before promotion. Incompatible or failed checks hold writes and public delivery; application rollback does not reverse committed data or migrations. A trusted operator's recovery hold must live outside restored Card/Business state and deny pages, aliases, images and management writes. Restore to isolated recovery services, establish actual relational/Auth/private-object coverage, then reconcile grants, membership/session revocations, post-snapshot Disable/transfer/unassignment, removed content/payment destinations, slug reservations, versions, history, audit, retry results and current image references. Missing trustworthy later evidence keeps affected routes/operations closed; do not fabricate lost history. Verify current sanitized bytes, distinguish orphans, use a fresh recovery cache namespace and prove session policy before deliberate reopening. This is a recommended procedure to rehearse, not a tested restore or guaranteed recovery target.

## Testing Decisions

The highest observable seam is **one application boundary**: drive authenticated NexTap Admin UI/private HTTP operations, then observe subsequent resource reads, read-only Card history, public Card/current-slug/alias HTML and gated Logo/Cover delivery. Browser-level checks cover navigation and rendered behavior; HTTP-level checks at the same boundary cover methods, payloads, headers, statuses, concurrency and retries that a browser cannot express precisely. This seam was reported to the parent through the Intercom supervision channel before this document was written; the report is validation evidence, not a human confirmation gate.

A good test asserts an externally meaningful contract: the page, destination, safe notice, image bytes or access denial a Visitor receives; the result, version and timeline an authorized Admin sees; and the absence of unintended state after rejection. It should survive internal module refactoring. Avoid assertions about helper calls, SQL text, cache method invocation counts or component layout that do not establish behavior. Cache/fault/time controls may arrange the scenario behind this boundary without making those implementation details the assertion surface.

The sources describe acceptance criteria, contracts and failure matrices, but no implemented application test harness or verified runtime tests. Therefore this seam is a proposed test organization, not a claim that an existing seam, framework, fixtures or similar executable tests were found. Documentation prior art is Product's acceptance criteria, API's route/status/retry tables, Security's failure/concurrency matrix, Database's constraints/transactions and Architecture's public-request, image and recovery sequences. No tests were run against a runtime, provider account, image decoder, migration or restore during this synthesis.

1. **Lifecycle and publication scenarios.** Exercise Disabled Business creation, Inactive/unassigned inventory, Assign without activation, separate Activate, Active plus Disabled denial, enablement with only mandatory fields, Deactivate retaining assignment, ordered transfer, and Unassign clearing it. Verify multiple Cards can resolve one Business, permanent Card URLs survive content/slug changes, aliases remain reserved, and Disable blocks Card/current/alias routes without altering Card state. Confirm the exact Inactive notice/status, unknown versus unavailable safe responses, direct Card rendering and eligible 308 aliases. Include invalid Active-unassigned state only as a defensive unavailable scenario, not a supported transition.

2. **Content and rendering scenarios.** Through management edits and subsequent public reads, cover each built-in Section, at-most-one cardinality, complete ordering including hidden members, persistence of order through toggles, Section-local Link membership, repeated-label rules, supported registries/icons and the 50-Link limit including concurrent inserts. Verify absence of hidden fields/Links and private metadata from rendered and serialized public output. Cover canonical contact derivation, clearing optional location/review fields, external payment/review navigation with no payment-success claims, all seven weekdays, absent versus all-closed schedules, overnight Sunday wrap, adjacency, overlap rejection and timezone synchronization. Include actual accepted limits and the labeled normalization defaults as boundary cases, exempting Card tokens from normalization.

3. **Freshness and cache scenarios.** Warm old content, then edit, Disable, transfer, change slug or replace/remove images; start new requests after commit and assert the new representation or denial on hit, miss and cache failure. Test canonical-to-alias changes during reads, coherent identity/revision changes and bounded retry/503 behavior, without allowing mixed content. Exercise HEAD, conditional requests, stale ETags, prefetch and optimized delivery to prove no bypass. A cache-write failure must still permit a valid current read; unavailable authoritative eligibility or required current content must deny rather than serve stale bytes. Do not assert recall of already delivered or authorized in-flight copies.

4. **Version, atomicity and retry scenarios.** Race tabs and lifecycle/content commands through HTTP. Verify only valid versioned transitions commit; rejected writes preserve all observable state and successful history. Inject required audit/history failure and confirm unavailable plus no committed mutation. Check Card no-ops leave versions/history unchanged. Repeat identical keys, conflict on changed scope/target/payload, race matching requests, revoke membership/MFA before replay, expire retention and lose the first response after commit. Read operation/resource/history to establish exactly one effect and committed truth. Cache/cleanup/delivery failure after commit must not masquerade as rollback. Any privileged inspection needed for minimized audit/retention assertions remains confined to persistence/provider verification; no new audit API is implied.

5. **Import scenarios.** Cover UTF-8 with and without BOM, exact headers, RFC quoting, blank lines, invalid encoding, whitespace/case-preserving tokens, Inactive-only status, file/row boundaries and full-file validation. Check malformed/duplicate files have no partial inventory effect, preview cannot authorize commit, expired/mismatched previews fail, existing tokens are skipped unchanged and concurrent imports remain safe. Verify import history belongs only to newly inserted Cards with the initiating Admin. Use synthetic tokens for prospective tests; compatibility with actual printed inventory remains blocked by the missing sample.

6. **Image and maintenance scenarios.** Upload actual bounded JPEG/PNG/WebP fixtures and verify sanitized delivery without retained metadata. Reject spoofed MIME/extensions, SVG, animation, malformed bytes, excessive dimensions/size and cross-Business references. Inject storage failure, uncertain upload completion, version conflict after upload, failure before reference commit, response loss after commit, cleanup failure and completion/cleanup races. Public old URLs must fail immediately after replacement/removal independent of physical deletion; the new/current object must survive cleanup. Prospective provider-backed maintenance checks verify temporary expiry and the proposed cleanup deadline, safe orphan handling and idempotent deletion. A unit test or mocked clock alone cannot prove provider deletion or scheduling feasibility.

7. **Identity and security scenarios.** Cover missing/expired session, authenticated non-Admin, wrong-project/invalid JWT, missing MFA, membership revocation before token expiry, 30-minute idle and 8-hour absolute limits, five-minute sensitive-action freshness and replay after freshness expires. Reject client roles/actor fields, cross-site or missing origin/CSRF evidence, and mismatched same-Business child references. Test privilege recovery boundaries without inventing a self-service recovery flow. Exercise stable error envelopes, no private-data leakage, pagination ordering/cursor tampering/query mismatch, request IDs and accepted rate limits including trusted proxy and dependency failure behavior.

8. **Necessary complementary provider probes.** Application success cannot establish that bypass routes are denied. Add direct external probes of Supabase Auth, Data and private Storage, exposed views/functions, grants/RLS and scoped privileged paths with anonymous, unrelated, revoked and authorized identities. Verify no public table/history/audit/retry metadata or reusable Storage URL bypass; authorized operations must still work within their intended scope. These are the minimal additional observable boundaries needed for isolation and private-image claims, not a separate test seam for every internal module. Their actual execution belongs to the runtime/provider gates.

9. **Dependency-failure and recovery evidence.** Inject unavailable Auth, database, eligibility/revision/current-image reads, Storage, rate-limit state and response transport behind the primary boundary, and assert safe denial or preserved committed truth as appropriate. Rehearse migration compatibility and recovery against disposable/staging provider services: hold outside restored state, reconcile post-snapshot revocation/history/retry evidence and private references, test old aliases/images/Card assignments and direct provider denial, and reopen only reconciled state. Once actual operating targets are selected, measure representative load, authoritative lookup cost, cache behavior, image processing/delivery and maintenance against provider quotas and recovery coverage. No budget, SLA, latency or recovery threshold is invented for these tests.

Together these scenarios cover the routing/authorization, Admin management, Card inventory/history, Business content/slug, Section/Link/hour, restricted projection/rendering/cache, image/maintenance and transaction/retry contracts through their outward behavior. Broad runtime and provider validation remains future evidence under the four gates below.

## Out of Scope

- Business Owner login, self-service dashboard, public signup, owner-account links and dormant owner relationships. Future ownership migration needs a separate product decision.
- Product scan/click counters, Visitor events, analytics APIs and an analytics data store. Minimal operational errors, routine security audit and required Card history remain in scope.
- Payment processing, balances, transaction confirmation, copyable wallet/payment identifiers or retries of external transactions. External HTTPS navigation does not attest a destination's trustworthiness.
- Synchronized review ratings/counts, arbitrary destination fetching, embedded-map fetching, coordinates as a new content model, holiday calendars and calculated open-now.
- A full website/page builder, arbitrary tenant HTML/scripts/components, unregistered Section types, icon URLs and an image gallery. Drag-and-drop ordering is not required.
- Card deletion, Card ID regeneration/reuse, Business hard deletion, reassignable former slugs, stale-eligibility grace periods, public raw/signed Storage access and purge-dependent publication jobs.
- Public JSON page APIs and unspecified route contracts. Logical Card creation support does not supply an invented single-Card creation endpoint.
- Microservices, Kubernetes, dedicated servers, multiple application databases, Redis clusters, complex queues, or newly selected Cloudflare D1/R2/KV/Durable Objects infrastructure. No additional image provider is selected.
- Implementation, provider provisioning, deployment, Git actions, tracker publication/labels or edits to the five source documents as part of this synthesis. This document does not authorize any of them.
